01 — Give Half Back

03

Chapter One

Give Half
Back

Wilson's central argument is disarmingly simple: protect half the planet and we protect the majority of its life. Not as sentiment, but as mathematics. The species-area relationship — a foundational principle of ecology — tells us that the amount of habitat directly determines how many species can survive within it.

This is not a proposal born of romanticism. It emerges from decades of fieldwork, from data gathered across hundreds of ecosystems on every continent. Half-Earth is, in Wilson's framing, the minimum viable threshold for planetary survival.

"Only by committing half the planet's surface to nature can we hope to save the immensity of life-forms that compose it."

02 — Imagine

Chapter Two

Imagine

Wilson closes Half-Earth with an act of imagination — a projection into a future where humanity has made the right choice. It is not utopian fantasy. It is a reasoned extrapolation from what we already know about ecological recovery.

Given protection and time, ecosystems restore themselves with remarkable speed. Wolves reintroduced to Yellowstone reshaped rivers. Mangroves replanted along coastlines rebuilt fish populations within a decade. Nature, if simply left alone, tends toward complexity and abundance.

"Imagine a world in which humanity has done the right thing. Half the planet is in reserve, the remainder managed wisely. Biodiversity loss has been halted."

Chapter Three

Our Planet's
Fight For Life

The sixth mass extinction is not a future event. It is underway. Species are disappearing at a rate estimated to be between one thousand and ten thousand times higher than the natural background rate — and accelerating.

Unlike the five previous mass extinctions, this one has a single cause: human activity. Habitat destruction, industrial agriculture, urban sprawl, pollution, and climate change have combined to produce a crisis with no precedent in the 3.8 billion year history of life on Earth.

"Animals and flowering plants are declining in number… almost entirely due to human activity. The pattern of human seizure of the planet can be summarised in a phrase: everything useful to us, nothing left for the rest."

05 — The Scale of Extinction

07

Chapter Five

The Scale
of Extinction

The numbers are almost impossible to comprehend. One million species currently face extinction — a figure that represents roughly one in eight of all known species on Earth. The rate of loss is accelerating, driven by the continued expansion of agriculture, urban development, and climate change.

What makes this crisis unlike any before it is not merely its scale, but its cause. Previous mass extinctions were the result of asteroid impacts or volcanic eruptions over millions of years. This one is the product of decisions made within a single human lifetime.

Wilson argues that the ethical weight of this fact is immense. We are the first species in the history of life on Earth to understand what we are doing — and to have the capacity to stop it.

"The extinction rate is now one thousand times greater than it was before the coming of humanity, and it is accelerating."

06 — The Science of Half-Earth

08

Chapter Six

The Science
of Half-Earth

The ecological foundation of Wilson's proposal rests on the species-area relationship — one of the most robust patterns in all of biology. First described in the 1960s, it states that as the area of a habitat decreases, the number of species it can support decreases predictably.

A ninety percent reduction in habitat area results in the loss of approximately fifty percent of species. Conversely, protecting fifty percent of the Earth's surface — chosen carefully to include the most biodiverse regions — could preserve the great majority of all known life.

"The key is the species-area relationship. To save most of the species, we must save most of the habitat."

Chapter Seven

A Vision for
the Future

Wilson's final chapter is an act of moral imagination. He asks us to picture a world fifty years from now in which the Half-Earth proposal has been enacted — not as an imposition, but as a collective choice made by a species that finally understood what was at stake.

"Imagine a world in which humanity has done the right thing. Half the planet is in reserve, the remainder managed wisely. Biodiversity loss has been halted. The great animal migrations continue. The Amazon remains intact. The Serengeti teems."

Our descendants would inherit a world richer in life than any human generation before them has known. That is not a utopia. It is a choice.
